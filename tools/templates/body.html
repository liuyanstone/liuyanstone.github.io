A searchable repository of __N__ studies on what digital technology actually does to growth, jobs, firms, welfare, inclusion, the public sector and the environment — organised by digital topic and by outcome, and sorted by how credibly each study identifies a causal effect. How the evidence is graded

The repository separates studies that can support a causal claim from those that document an association. Both are useful — but only the first two categories below license statements of the form “X caused Y to change by Z”.

Sources and method. Entries are drawn from peer-reviewed economics journals, NBER and CEPR working papers, World Bank Policy Research Working Papers, and publications of the IMF, OECD, ILO, ITU and allied agencies, together with working papers circulated on SSRN and arXiv. The grading reflects the identification strategy the authors themselves use.

Caveats. A strong design does not make an estimate universally valid: most entries identify a local effect for one population, one technology generation and one period. Magnitudes are reported as stated in the source. Where a study reports many estimates, the entry gives the headline result. Follow the link on each entry back to the paper before citing a number.
